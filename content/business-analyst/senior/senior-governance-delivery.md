Senior BAs often work across several teams that each have their own backlog, delivery rhythm and local priorities. The job is to keep decisions, outcomes and dependencies coherent without creating a second project bureaucracy.

## Set proportionate governance

> **In short:** Define the decisions, owners, evidence and forums the initiative actually needs.

Governance is the system for making and recording decisions, managing risk and maintaining accountability. Define decision types, thresholds, owners, required evidence, escalation paths and review cadence. Use existing forums where they work.

Do not route every detail to a steering committee. Teams should decide reversible implementation details within agreed guardrails; sponsors decide investment and scope trade-offs; control owners decide acceptable control design. Publish that boundary.

Maintain a decision log with date, owner, options, rationale, conditions and follow-up. Reopen a decision when its assumptions materially change, not because someone prefers another outcome.

## Coordinate cross-team dependencies

> **In short:** Surface dependencies early and assign an owner on both sides.

A dependency is an output or decision one team needs from another. Capture what is needed, provider and consumer owners, target date, acceptance condition, impact if late and escalation route.

In the reconciliation case, delivery may depend on a regional data feed change, central identity roles, ledger interface capacity and control approval. A line on a RAID log is not enough; confirm both teams agree on the deliverable and date.

Map critical-path and sequencing dependencies. Revisit them during planning and after changes. Escalate early with options such as resequencing, reducing scope or adding capacity.

## Lead portfolio risk and escalation

> **In short:** Prioritize risk by exposure, trigger and response owner, not by how dramatic it sounds.

For each risk, state the uncertain event, cause, consequence, likelihood or exposure, trigger, response, owner and review date. Separate risks from current issues and assumptions. Quantify impact where possible.

Senior BAs connect risks across workstreams: a late feed change may delay a control test and miss a close window. Bring material risks to the correct decision forum with evidence, options and a recommendation.

Escalation is not blame. It is a timely request for authority, resources or a trade-off beyond the team's control. Record the decision and communicate it to affected teams.

## Align delivery across methods

> **In short:** Keep outcomes and interfaces consistent even when teams use different delivery methods.

A portfolio may combine Scrum, Kanban, vendor delivery, regulatory milestones and operational change. Align on shared outcomes, interface contracts, decision dates, quality evidence and integration points. Do not force identical ceremonies when teams have different needs.

For agile teams, keep the outcome and cross-team dependencies visible in backlog refinement. For plan-driven work, manage baselines and approved changes. For hybrid programs, make the handoff and evidence expectations explicit.

A senior BA helps translate between work-item detail and portfolio decisions. Report progress based on verified outcomes and risks, not the number of stories or documents completed.

## Govern scope and change

> **In short:** Assess change against value, risk and downstream impact before accepting it.

A request to add scope can affect architecture, data migration, controls, testing, training, regulatory evidence, support and benefits. Ask what need it addresses, what priority it has, what it displaces and whether the change is urgent or can be sequenced.

Use a clear change path proportionate to delivery style. In agile, ordering the backlog is still a decision; it does not remove impact analysis or stakeholder alignment. In regulated work, preserve approvals and traceability.

Maintain a baseline only where it supports control or agreement. Avoid paperwork for low-risk choices, but record consequential changes and their rationale.

## Maintain auditability and vendor accountability

> **In short:** Preserve enough evidence to show who decided what, why, and how obligations are met.

For regulated or outsourced work, identify applicable controls, contractual responsibilities, data handling, service levels, audit rights and exit obligations with legal, compliance and procurement partners. The BA does not give legal advice but ensures these needs are analyzed and owned.

Keep traceability from obligation to requirement, test, approval and operational evidence. Confirm vendor deliverables are measurable and acceptance criteria are agreed before work starts.

Plan for access, retention, incident notification, continuity and data return or deletion where relevant. Record exceptions and approved risk acceptance; do not imply that a vendor owns the organization’s accountability.
